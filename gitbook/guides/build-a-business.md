# Build a Business on Suwappu

Suwappu is most useful to builders as a **financial action plane**: you add a customer-facing product, strategy, workflow, or intelligence layer; Suwappu supplies discovery, quotes, simulation, execution primitives, and agent controls.

The goal is not to promise that a strategy makes money. The goal is to build something customers will pay for and to measure its economics honestly.

## Pick the value you own

Three business models fit the current APIs well.

### 1. Paid intelligence

Sell information or decisions without taking custody:

- portfolio analysis and rebalance recommendations;
- perps liquidation-risk or market alerts;
- lending-rate/risk monitors;
- prediction-market research and calibration;
- route, price, or cross-chain opportunity alerts.

This is the simplest boundary because the customer can pay you for the insight while Suwappu stays read-only or quote-only. Start here if you do not need to move funds.

Good examples to extend are the [Flywheel strategy/product reference](flywheel.md), [MCP Portfolio Advisor](mcp-portfolio-advisor.md), [LangChain integration](langchain.md), [CrewAI integration](crewai.md), [Perpetual Futures Research](perps-trading.md), and the public Suwappu example repositories.

### 2. Automation SaaS

Sell an ongoing workflow: DCA, rebalancing, policy-aware execution, reporting, or strategy automation. Give each customer their own agent/key and keep their state isolated.

A production automation should have this progression:

```text
observe -> decide -> quote -> simulate -> approve/policy gate -> execute -> reconcile
```

Charge a subscription, usage tier, or explicit automation fee. Keep live execution behind spend caps, wallet policies, idempotency keys, audit events, and a kill switch. See [Strategy Lifecycle](strategy-lifecycle.md) before enabling unattended capital.

### 3. Distribution and referrals

Suwappu's Telegram product has a referral program in which eligible referrers currently receive a share of fees generated by referred users. That is a **product referral program**, not a generic Agent API monetization parameter; terms, eligibility, and payout behavior can change, so confirm the current in-product terms before modeling it as revenue.

The Agent API does **not** currently expose a general `builder_fee`, `integrator_fee`, or referral-attribution field that lets any third-party app automatically collect a fee from Agent API order flow. Suwappu itself uses venue-specific builder/referral mechanisms in some first-party integrations, but those are not a public builder contract. Do not silently add an execution surcharge and assume Suwappu will collect it for you.

If flow-based monetization matters to your product, use an explicit customer billing agreement today and treat a future Suwappu builder-attribution API as a separate capability when it is actually documented.

## Understand the x402 boundary

Suwappu's x402 support is a way for an agent to pay **Suwappu** for metered API/tool usage. Those payments are your input cost, not your customer revenue.

You can expose your own paid API or agent using a payment system such as x402, Stripe, or another billing provider, but that is your service and ledger. Keep the two cash flows distinct:

```text
customer -> your product -> your revenue ledger
                       |
                       +-> Suwappu / model / infra / chain costs
```

## Track builder margin separately from trading P&L

A builder can have profitable customer economics even when a customer's strategy has a losing month, and the reverse can also be true. Never collapse them into one number.

For the business:

```text
builder margin
  = customer subscription + usage revenue + verified referral revenue
  - Suwappu/API cost
  - model/provider cost
  - infrastructure cost
  - gas/venue costs you subsidize
  - support, refunds, and credits
```

For a customer's strategy:

```text
strategy net P&L
  = realized gains/losses + mark-to-market change
  - venue fees - gas - bridge fees - realized slippage
```

Store both ledgers. A quote's expected output is neither business revenue nor realized strategy profit.

## A practical pricing worksheet

Before choosing a price, measure a realistic month for one active customer:

| Input | Example question |
|-------|------------------|
| Suwappu usage | How many quotes, simulations, reads, and executions will this customer generate? |
| Model cost | How many reasoning/tool turns are needed per useful outcome? |
| Infrastructure | Scheduler, database, queues, monitoring, alert delivery, support |
| Chain costs | Which costs are customer-paid versus subsidized by you? |
| Failure/refund budget | What happens when an upstream venue, RPC, or model is unavailable? |
| Target margin | What gross margin leaves room to support and improve the product? |

Then price from observed cost, not from a hoped-for trading return.

## Three products you can ship

### Portfolio copilot

- **Free:** allocation and drift report.
- **Paid:** continuous monitoring, custom target bands, alerts, saved policies, and audit history.
- **Execution:** optional, explicit, and gated after a simulation.

### Strategy automation

- **Free:** backtest/replay report.
- **Paid:** paper deployment, scheduled operation, performance ledger, alerts, and eventually a capped live tier.
- **Execution:** only after the exact same decision path passes paper mode.

### Market intelligence API

- **Free:** delayed or limited watchlist.
- **Paid:** lower-latency alerts, custom rules, historical context, exports/webhooks, or a customer-facing MCP tool.
- **Execution:** unnecessary unless it adds real customer value.

## Production checklist

Before charging for an automation product, make sure you can answer all of these:

- **Authority:** What can the agent read, prepare, sign, or broadcast?
- **Version:** Which SDK/package/API contract did this release test against?
- **Preview:** Can a customer see the quote, minimum output, costs, and warnings first?
- **Replay:** Can you reproduce why a decision happened?
- **Paper mode:** Does the live strategy run without moving funds?
- **Accounting:** Do you store fills and costs instead of estimating profit from entry quotes?
- **Controls:** Are there per-action/daily caps, approvals, idempotency, and a kill switch?
- **Operations:** What retries safely, what must never retry blindly, and how do you recover after a crash?
- **Observability:** Can the customer see status, errors, and a reconciled transaction history?
- **Billing:** Are customer revenue and Suwappu/venue/model costs separate ledgers?

That checklist is the contract the example repositories in this organization are converging on. Start with [Strategy Lifecycle](strategy-lifecycle.md) for the strategy side and the [API Reference](../api-reference/README.md) for the current action surface.

> Building a business is not a guarantee of investment returns. If your product gives financial advice, takes custody, or serves regulated users, get appropriate legal/compliance guidance for the jurisdictions you serve.
