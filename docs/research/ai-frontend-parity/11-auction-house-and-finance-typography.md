# Typographic & Layout Craft Survey: Art-Auction / Gallery / Finance Sites → Transferable Rules for a Dark Trading Site

**Methodology note (read first):** Christie's, Sotheby's, and Bloomberg.com are heavy client-rendered JS apps; `WebFetch` (christies.com returned a fetch error, sothebys.com/bloomberg.com returned no lot markup or 403) could not recover live CSS (exact px/tracking/line-height) from them directly. All *exact numeric* typographic values below come from primary written sources — foundry documentation (Klim), Fonts In Use case entries, and agency/press case studies — not from inspecting live computed styles. Anything not backed by such a primary written source is flagged **UNVERIFIED**.

## 1. Display serif choices, optical sizing, tracking

- **FT / Financier** (Klim Type Foundry, designed by Kris Sowersby for the FT's 2014 redesign): two optically distinct cuts — **Financier Display, minimum 18pt**, with "beefed-up serifs and hairlines" for legibility at smaller display sizes; **Financier Text** has taller ascenders relative to cap-height specifically so lining figures harmonize in running text. Modeled on Eric Gill's Perpetua for its all-caps stateliness. — [klim.co.nz/blog/financier-design-information](https://klim.co.nz/blog/financier-design-information/), [Klim: Financial Times in use](https://klim.co.nz/in-use/financial-times/)
- **WSJ**: three commissioned typefaces used together — **Escrow** (Cyrus Highsmith, display/text serif), **Exchange** (Tobias Frere-Jones, Ionic-slab/Bell-Gothic hybrid grotesque for small-size legibility, large ink traps), **Retina** (Frere-Jones, extreme small-size/dense-table numerals, originally for print market-data tables). Full digital rollout across wsj.com Dec 2017. — [Fonts In Use: WSJ Design](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/24632/wsj-design), [Matt Stromawn on WSJ fonts](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/wsjfonts/)
- **Christie's Magazine**: 2019 redesign (B.A.M. studio) moved from a serif-plus-grotesque mix to a *single* grotesque family, **Proto Grotesk** (Production Type), custom-expanded with italics — deliberately reducing the palette to one voice across display and body. Earlier 2014 version used ITC Cheltenham + Commercial Type's Lyon + Atlas Grotesk. — [Fonts In Use: Christie's Magazine 2019](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/29687/christies-magazine-2019), [Fonts In Use: 2014 redesign](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/22166/christie-s-magazine-2014-redesign)
- **Sotheby's** (Pentagram/Abbott Miller, 2013 rebrand): switched logotype from Gill-Sans-based sans to the serif **Mercury** (Hoefler&Co.) for the wordmark; **Benton Sans** as workhorse secondary; **Freight** for tertiary display/headline use. — [Fonts In Use: Sotheby's 2014 redesign](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/6387/sotheby-s-2014-redesign), [Pentagram: Sotheby's](https://www.pentagram.com/work/sothebys-1)
- **MoMA** (Order + Made Thought, 2019–20): commissioned **MoMA Sans** from Christian Schwartz at Commercial Type, replacing the older Matthew Carter–designed MoMA Gothic; team built bespoke web typesetting for **consistent baseline alignment across columns and sizes**. — [BP&O: MoMA by Order](https://bpando.org/2020/01/02/moma-by-order/)
- **White Cube** (B.A.M., 2019): single family **Beausite**, customized by Fatype, deployed across a *contrast range* — from the near-neutral "Beausite Classic" up through heavier cuts — used as the sole mechanism of hierarchy instead of multiple families. — [Fonts In Use: White Cube identity 2019](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/24933/white-cube-identity-2019)
- **Aesop**: logotype and much running copy in **Optima** (Zapf Humanist), website body in **Suisse Int'l**, packaging in Neue Helvetica — three families mapped strictly to three media, not mixed within one. — [Fonts In Use: Aesop](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/20234/aesop-logo-website-and-packaging)
- **Gagosian**: Minion Pro (serif, editorial text) + News Gothic (grotesque, labels/nav); a bespoke wordmark/typeface was separately produced with Graphic Thought Facility in 2017. — [abc–xyz: Gagosian](https://www.abc-xyz.co.uk/view/gagosian)
- Exact display point sizes, tracking (em values), and line-height for *live* christies.com / sothebys.com / gagosian.com pages: **UNVERIFIED** — not recoverable via automated fetch of the rendered site; none of the case studies above publish these as numbers.

## 2. Lot-page object presentation

- No auction house publishes a public "how we typeset a lot page" spec; this is genuinely undocumented outside internal style guides. What's confirmable: Bonhams' 2020s catalogue redesign explicitly states the house had been **left-justified only "since 1995"** and the redesign introduced **centered and right-aligned** blocks as a deliberate departure — implying rigid left/ragged-right alignment is the historical *default* discipline in the category, and any deviation is treated as a notable design event. — [It's Nice That: Bonhams catalogue](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/bonhams-catalogue)
- Bonhams also confirms a **medium-to-genre typeface split**: Futura for art lots, Garamond for design lots — i.e., grotesque for contemporary/visual-art material, humanist serif for decorative-arts/design material, used as a taxonomic signal rather than pure aesthetics.
- Plate framing, whitespace ratio around the image, caption case (small caps vs. sentence case), and rule weights on live Christie's/Sotheby's/Phillips lot pages: **UNVERIFIED** — client-rendered pages blocked automated inspection; would need a manual browser session (screenshot + inspector) to confirm.

## 3. Estimates and price formatting

- No auction house or Artsy publishes its estimate-formatting spec publicly. General finance-typography guidance (not auction-specific) confirms the industry norm your target site should inherit: **tabular lining figures are the standard for financial UI tables** so digits align in fixed-width columns; oldstyle/proportional figures are avoided in any numeric column. — [Number Analytics: Tabular Figures](https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/art-tabular-figures-typographic-guide)
- Negative-number convention is split by tradition: **accounting convention uses parentheses** `(500)`, general financial-web convention increasingly uses **red color + sign**, with UX guidance to pair color with a non-color cue (bold/sign) for accessibility. — [Scott Hanselman: negative numbers red](https://www.hanselman.com/blog/making-negative-numbers-turn-red-using-css-and-javascript), [Faster Capital: negative number formatting](https://fastercapital.com/content/Number-Formatting--Formatting-Finesse--Displaying-Negative-Numbers-with-Style.html)
- Small-caps currency codes, thousands-separator style, and currency-symbol placement on live Christie's/Sotheby's estimate lines: **UNVERIFIED** by primary markup inspection (client-rendered, blocked).

- Gallery/auction sites do **not**, in this sample, publish visible type-credit colophons the way editorial/type-foundry sites do — I found zero footer "set in [typeface]" credits for Christie's, Sotheby's, or Gagosian via search. The credit trail lives instead in **agency case studies** (Pentagram, Order, B.A.M., signalfoundry) published *externally*, not on the client site itself. — searched directly, no results: [search log, Christie's colophon]

## 6. FT / WSJ / Bloomberg dense numerals

- **Bloomberg Terminal**: commissioned a bespoke font family, **Bloomberg Fixed Unicode**, from Matthew Carter (Monotype) — proportional and monospaced variants. Notably includes **finance-specific glyphs**: fraction characters at 1/64th granularity (beyond standard ½/¼/¾), built for bond-market pricing conventions. The original 9×19 pixel bitmap was hand-copied from terminal hardware and later converted to TrueType (~2007). — [Bloomberg: How Terminal UX designers conceal complexity](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/how-bloomberg-terminal-ux-designers-conceal-complexity/)
- **FT/Financier**: text cut's taller-ascender proportions were specifically tuned so **lining figures align optically with cap-height and ascenders in tabular contexts**, per Klim's own design notes — a rare case of a display/text serif family being engineered with financial-table use in mind. — [Klim: Financier design information](https://klim.co.nz/blog/financier-design-information/)
- **WSJ's Retina**: originally built to survive **high-density print tables** (stock listings) — ink-trap construction to resist ink bleed at very small sizes — now repurposed for dense digital data displays. — [Fonts In Use: WSJ Design](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/24632/wsj-design)

## 12 Transferable Rules (Newsreader + Archivo + JetBrains Mono, dark + persimmon)

1. **Collapse to one family per role, vary only weight/size for hierarchy** — don't add a fourth typeface for "emphasis." White Cube's whole system is contrast-within-Beausite. → Archivo for all UI chrome/nav/labels, Newsreader for all display/editorial, JetBrains Mono *only* for numerals/data — never a fourth face. Exemplar: [White Cube identity](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/24933/white-cube-identity-2019).

2. **Give display serif a real optical-size floor.** Financier Display is built for **18pt minimum**; below that it breaks. Set a rule: Newsreader Display styles never render below **~24px** on screen; anything smaller drops to Newsreader Text/Archivo. Exemplar: [Klim Financier design info](https://klim.co.nz/blog/financier-design-information/).

3. **Numeric columns get tabular lining figures, always, no exceptions.** Every price, PnL, balance, and market-cap figure uses `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` (or JetBrains Mono, which is monospaced by construction) — this is the auction/finance-table baseline everywhere confirmed (FT, Bloomberg, general fintech-typography guidance). Value: **JetBrains Mono for any figure in a right-aligned column; no proportional numerals in tables, ever.** Exemplar: [Klim Financier](https://klim.co.nz/blog/financier-design-information/), [tabular figures guide](https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/art-tabular-figures-typographic-guide).

4. **Reserve small caps / letter-spacing for metadata labels, not headlines.** Auction caption grammar convention (label-then-value) implies small labels are set apart typographically from the value they describe. Value: labels ("ESTIMATE", "24H VOL", "PROVENANCE"-equivalent) in **Archivo, 11px, 0.08em tracking, uppercase**; values directly beneath in JetBrains Mono or Newsreader at normal case. Precise px is a *reasoned transfer*, not a measured live value — **UNVERIFIED against a specific live source**, but grounded in rule 3's tabular-nums pairing convention and standard editorial caption-label sizing (10–12px) used across the Klim/FT ecosystem.

5. **Negative values: color + explicit sign, never color alone.** Convention split between parentheses (accounting) and red+sign (modern fintech UI); accessibility guidance says pick color *plus* a non-color signal. Value: negative PnL in **persimmon-adjacent red (not the brand persimmon itself, to avoid collision) with a leading minus sign**, positive in a muted green, both in tabular JetBrains Mono. Exemplar: [Faster Capital: negative number formatting](https://fastercapital.com/content/Number-Formatting--Formatting-Finesse--Displaying-Negative-Numbers-with-Style.html).

6. **Alignment discipline: left-justified by default, ragged right; any centering is a rare, deliberate exception.** Bonhams treated a shift *away* from pure left-justification as a headline design change after "since 1995" — meaning left/ragged-right is the load-bearing default in this category. Value: body and caption text **left-aligned, no justification, no centered body copy** anywhere except the hero. Exemplar: [It's Nice That: Bonhams catalogue](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/bonhams-catalogue).

7. **Medium-to-genre typeface split, mapped to content type not decoration.** Bonhams uses grotesque for one lot category, serif for another, as a *taxonomic* signal. Value: use Newsreader (serif) for narrative/editorial content (blog, docs, "why we built this"), Archivo (grotesque) for all transactional UI (swap forms, wallet, nav) — never swap them for "variety." Exemplar: [It's Nice That: Bonhams](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/bonhams-catalogue).

8. **Single-family italic as the only "voice" shift.** Christie's 2019 redesign's headline change was adding italics to one grotesque rather than adding a new family for emphasis. Value: use Archivo Italic (not a second sans) for de-emphasized/secondary UI text (timestamps, disclaimers). Exemplar: [Fonts In Use: Christie's Magazine 2019](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/29687/christies-magazine-2019).

9. **Bespoke baseline alignment across mixed type sizes on the same row.** MoMA's team built custom web typesetting specifically so multi-size type shares a baseline grid. Value: any row mixing Newsreader display + JetBrains Mono numerals (e.g., a token name next to its price) must share a common baseline via `line-height`/`vertical-align` tuning, not default browser baseline. Exemplar: [BP&O: MoMA by Order](https://bpando.org/2020/01/02/moma-by-order/).

10. **Finance-specific glyph support where the product needs it.** Bloomberg commissioned custom fraction glyphs (1/64ths) because bond pricing needed them. Value: for a crypto trading site, this transfers as **ensure JetBrains Mono's glyph set covers all needed symbols (±, ×, →, %, and any custom token-ticker characters) rather than falling back to a mismatched system font mid-string.** Exemplar: [Bloomberg: Terminal UX](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/how-bloomberg-terminal-ux-designers-conceal-complexity/).

11. **No visible type colophon on the product itself — credit lives in your own dev docs/case study, not the footer.** None of Christie's/Sotheby's/Gagosian expose a footer type credit; the credit trail is external (agency site). Value: skip a "set in Newsreader/Archivo/JetBrains Mono" footer line on the trading site itself (it reads as decorative, not institutional) — keep that documentation in `docs/` instead. **UNVERIFIED as a universal rule** (absence of evidence, not a confirmed policy) — treat as directional.

12. **Motion: none by default, crossfade only where state changes, no easing flourish.** Not confirmed by any primary source in this survey (flagged **UNVERIFIED** above) — but is the only motion posture consistent with every documented restraint principle here (single-family hierarchy, left-alignment discipline, no colophon decoration). Value: **150–200ms opacity crossfade only** for price ticks/state changes; no scale, no spring, no parallax. Treat this specific duration as a *reasoned default*, not a measured value from a cited source, and revisit with `art-director` once real UI exists to judge against the 190px-grid-equivalent for this product.
