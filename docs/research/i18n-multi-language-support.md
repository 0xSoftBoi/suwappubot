## 1. Executive Summary

- **Externalize every string to a stable key, never concatenate fragments.** Airbnb (1M+ "pieces of content" across 62 languages at 100B+ translate requests/day, per a **verified** primary-source Airbnb Engineering post — see freshness caveat below), Slack (~20,000 strings across ~2,000 files, **verified** verbatim), and Shopify all enforce this as the foundational rule — concatenation breaks in SOV languages and blocks pluralization/gender handling. [Airbnb Eng](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/building-airbnbs-internationalization-platform-45cf0104b63c), [Slack Eng](https://slack.engineering/localizing-slack/)
  - **Freshness caveat (new in v2):** the Airbnb figures are from an **October 2020** post — ~5.75 years old as of today. The primary source's exact wording is "1 million pieces of content," not "phrases" (same order of magnitude, different word). Treat "1M+/100B+" as an illustrative historical data point, not Airbnb's current scale.
- **ICU MessageFormat is the de facto standard** for plurals/gender/interpolation (react-intl, Vue-i18n, Android, iOS, Shopify Polaris all use it); a successor, MessageFormat 2, reached Unicode/CLDR-stable status in **CLDR 47 (March 2025)** but **no major production i18n library defaults to it yet** — see Appendix A for why "not yet the safe default" is now a firmer, dated conclusion rather than a hedge. [Crowdin ICU guide](https://crowdin.com/blog/icu-guide), [locize — MF2 in i18next](https://locize.com/blog/messageformat-2-i18next)
- **Telegram gives language for free but it's unreliable** — `language_code` is optional, sometimes malformed (`it` vs `it-IT`), and never auto-translates messages; every serious bot/messaging platform layers an **explicit stored override** on top of the live-detected signal. [Telegram Bot Features](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features)
- **Never hand-roll number/currency formatting** — use CLDR-backed `Intl.NumberFormat`/equivalents; crypto adds a unique failure mode on top of standard fintech risk (8–18 decimal assets, MAX-button truncation bugs where displayed value diverges from on-chain submitted value — **confirmed** as a real production bug, not a hypothetical). [CLDR patterns](https://cldr.unicode.org/translation/number-currency-formats/number-and-currency-patterns), [DeFiChain #176 — verified](https://github.com/BirthdayResearch/defichain-app/issues/176)
- **Continuous localization is now a commodity workflow**: webhook detects new strings → push to TMS or inline AI action → MT/LLM first draft → human review → PR merge → CI. All five major TMS vendors (Crowdin, Lokalise, Phrase, Smartling, Transifex) converge on this loop; a lightweight "AI-in-CI" variant exists for teams that don't want a paid platform yet — **new in v2: independent LLM-vs-NMT quality research (§2.3.2) confirms this pattern is now sound, not just cheap**, provided an automated placeholder/ICU-token validation step is added before human review. [Crowdin GitHub Action](https://github.com/crowdin/github-action), [Localhero.ai](https://localhero.ai/blog/automate-i18n-github-actions)
- **Regulated financial/legal copy is a distinct translation tier** from marketing/UI copy — treat KYC, disclosures, and T&Cs with certified translators, glossary, and TM. **New in v2**: this is no longer just a general fintech-localization claim — EU MiCA's uneven national application is now backed by a **primary regulator source** (a joint AMF/FMA/CONSOB position paper), and crypto exchanges show concrete precedent for translation-quality risk (§2.4, §5). [AMF/FMA/CONSOB position paper](https://www.consob.it/documents/d/asset-library-1912910/consob_amf_fma_position_paper_mica_20250915), [Centific](https://www.centific.com/blog/financial-services-localization-compliance)
- **Pseudo-localization in CI catches expansion/RTL bugs before real translation starts** — Netflix, Slack, and Android all build this into their pipelines; translated text can expand up to ~35–40%, and layout/mirroring bugs are otherwise invisible until a human translator (or real user) hits them. **New in v2**: Netflix's pseudo-localization is the **default debug-build language (opt-out, not opt-in)**, not merely an available tool — a meaningfully stronger practice than "available if you remember to enable it." [Netflix TechBlog — Pseudo-Localization](https://netflixtechblog.com/pseudo-localization-netflix-12fff76fbcbe), [Android pseudolocales](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/pseudolocales)

## 2. How Top Companies Do It

### 2.1 Enterprise i18n Architecture

- **String externalization at scale**: Airbnb runs three parallel extraction paths (service config, static source analysis, runtime access-pattern logging) feeding a platform serving 1M+ pieces of content / 100B+ requests/day at microsecond-to-sub-ms latency (two related latency claims appear in the primary source: "microseconds" at the platform tier, "sub-millisecond" for cached client-side requests). This is from an **October 2020** post — dated, verified, not current-day. Slack wraps ~20,000 strings across 2,000 files in `{t}` template blocks, extracted via static analysis and enforced through collaborative "string jam" events. [Airbnb Eng](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/building-airbnbs-internationalization-platform-45cf0104b63c), [Slack Eng](https://slack.engineering/localizing-slack/)
- **Plural/gender via ICU MessageFormat**: one translatable string encodes locale-specific plural rules — `{count, plural, one {...} other {...}}` — rather than branching in code. Shopify Polaris and Slack both standardize on ICU; Slack's own blog **explicitly confirms** (verified verbatim) it built custom ICU-syntax validation tooling because "[ICU] was also not directly supported by any Translation Management System (TMS) we could find," so they had to "turn off most of the TMS's built-in parsing and validation ... and do that ourselves." [Slack Eng](https://slack.engineering/localizing-slack/), [Polaris i18n](https://polaris-react.shopify.com/foundations/internationalization)
- **Explicit fallback chains**: Airbnb falls back to a parent locale per a predetermined chain on missing keys; Android resolves `pt-rBR → pt → en` via resource qualifiers. Slack hashes the *source English string* as the translation key (with a `fallback_hash`) so minor copy edits don't orphan existing translations. [Android docs](https://developer.android.com/training/basics/supporting-devices/languages), [Slack Eng](https://slack.engineering/localizing-slack/)
- **RTL**: Material Design 3 defines explicit mirroring rules — layout/icons/sliders mirror, numerals and non-directional icons must not — exposed via Android's `autoMirrored` and start/end layout attributes; full native RTL layout landed in Android 4.2 (API 17). [Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/bidirectionality-rtl), [Android — supporting languages](https://developer.android.com/training/basics/supporting-devices/languages) (UNVERIFIED: no primary source found describing Slack/Airbnb/Netflix's own RTL implementation specifics.)
- **Pseudo-localization QA gate**: Netflix runs pseudo-localization server-side in its Global String Repository before strings reach any client, and — per v2 deep-dive — it's the **default debug-build language**, not opt-in. Slack's accented pseudolocale expands strings ~35% while preserving HTML/emoji. Android ships build-time `en-XA` (expanded) and `ar-XB` (RTL) pseudolocales. [Netflix TechBlog](https://netflixtechblog.com/pseudo-localization-netflix-12fff76fbcbe), [Android pseudolocales](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/pseudolocales)
- **Native `Intl` for dates/numbers/currency**: V8/Chrome built ECMA-402 `Intl` directly into the JS engine (not a JS-based extension) for locale-correct formatting without app-level logic, requiring ICU ≥ v63 as of V8 7.2; rewriting `Intl.NumberFormat` construction in C++ made it ~24x faster. Spotify separately widened UI layout tolerances across 74 languages because translated text can grow up to 35%. [V8 blog](https://v8.dev/blog/intl), [Spotify Eng](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2022/09/scaling-translations-at-spotify)

#### 2.1.1 New in v2 — Google: the substrate everyone else builds on (ICU/CLDR/Android/V8)

Google doesn't just *use* i18n infrastructure like the other case studies — as steward of ICU-adjacent standards, it *is* the infrastructure underneath much of this report's other examples (V8's `Intl`, Android's plural/RTL system, and CLDR itself, which every ICU MessageFormat implementation depends on).

- ICU began as IBM's "IBM Classes for Unicode" (open-sourced 1999), but in **May 2016 joined the Unicode Consortium** as technical committee ICU-TC; Unicode co-founder and CTO **Mark Davis** (a long-time Google internationalization architect) chairs the related CLDR Technical Committee, which supplies the locale data (plural rules, date/number patterns) that ICU, Android, and V8 all consume. ICU now ships as a standard Windows component since Windows 10 (1703) — i.e., genuinely cross-industry, not Google-proprietary. [icu.unicode.org](https://icu.unicode.org/), [ICU — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Components_for_Unicode), [CLDR-TC](https://cldr.unicode.org/cldr-tc)
- **Android's public i18n API** is `values-<lang>` resource folders + `<plurals>` resources implementing CLDR's six-category plural model via `getQuantityString()`, plus `android:supportsRtl="true"` + `android:autoMirrored="true"` for RTL icon mirroring. [Android — string resources](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/string-resource)
- **V8/Chrome's `Intl`** (ECMA-402) is built directly on ICU, and Google's Internationalization team has co-extended ICU itself (new `Intl.RelativeTimeFormat`/`Intl.ListFormat`/`Intl.Locale` APIs, ICU bug fixes) — improvements that flow back into other engines, e.g. Firefox. [v8.dev/blog/intl](https://v8.dev/blog/intl)
- UNVERIFIED: no public primary source documents Google's *internal* Translation Console/TMS tooling with concrete scale numbers — distinct from the public-facing Play Console App Translation Service (29 languages, machine-translated store listings), which is the only Google-branded translation tool with published specifics. [Play Console App Translation service](https://support.google.com/l10n/answer/6341304)
- **Suwappu relevance**: this is background/foundational, not a direct product comparable — but it reinforces Appendix A's recommendation to build on CLDR-plural-category semantics rather than inventing a custom plural scheme, since that's the same substrate Android/V8/ICU all share.

### 2.2 Messaging / Chat-Bot Specific Patterns (most relevant to Suwappu's Telegram bot)

- **Auto-detect, then override**: Telegram's `User.language_code` is optional and format-inconsistent; bots must fall back to a stored preference, then to a hard default. WhatsApp Business API doesn't auto-translate at all — templates carry an explicit `language` field the business sets per send. Discord natively supports per-locale command `localizations` maps plus a runtime `interaction.locale`. [Telegram Bot Features](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features), [Meta WhatsApp templates](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/business-management-api/message-templates/), [Discord — Application Commands](https://docs.discord.com/developers/interactions/application-commands)
- **Standard override pattern**: a per-user language DB column + a `/language`-style command with an inline-keyboard picker, with the stored value always taking priority over auto-detected locale on every subsequent message. CryptoBot exposes this under Settings > Language for 4 languages — directly comparable to Suwappu's bot UX. [medium.com/@avis](https://medium.com/@avis/crypto-bot-telegram-bot-features-1cbd7f17657d)
- **Template systems**: mature Telegram-bot frameworks (aiogram) ship built-in gettext/Fluent middleware (`aiogram.utils.i18n`); simpler bots (Suwappu's current approach) use a flat dict keyed by string-id → lang → template with `.format()` interpolation and English fallback. [aiogram docs](https://docs.aiogram.dev/en/stable/utils/i18n.html)
- **Buttons/keyboards use the same lookup**: `InlineKeyboardButton` labels are resolved from the translation dict at keyboard-build time, not inside callback logic. [core.telegram.org/api/bots/buttons](https://core.telegram.org/api/bots/buttons)
- **RTL is not automatic** in Telegram or Discord clients — developers must manually order button rows/text for Arabic/Hebrew, and Markdown rendering can interact badly with RTL numeral/punctuation ordering. [Hebrew alignment fix](https://medium.com/@python-javascript-php-html-css/fixing-hebrew-text-alignment-in-telegram-bot-api-e951f9039b72)

#### 2.2.1 New in v2 — Meta / WhatsApp deep dive

- **FBT (Meta's own i18n library)**: two Babel transforms — `babel-plugin-fbt` turns `<fbt>`/`fbt(...)` JSX into hashed-phrase runtime calls, and a companion collector extracts all phrases at build time into a translator-facing JSON manifest. At runtime, FBT builds "tables of all possible variations" (gender/number/pronoun) per phrase rather than a flat key→string map — a materially different model from i18next/react-intl's per-key lookup, built specifically to keep translatable text inline with surrounding JSX and to handle grammatical correctness (plurals, gender, enums) that flat-key libraries handle poorly. [facebookincubator/fbt](https://github.com/facebookincubator/fbt), [fbt transform docs](https://github.com/facebook/fbt/blob/main/docs/transform.md), [facebook.github.io/fbt](https://facebook.github.io/fbt/). UNVERIFIED: no primary Meta source explicitly contrasts FBT against i18next/react-intl by name — rationale above is inferred from FBT's stated design goals.
- **WhatsApp Business API templates are localized as fully separate objects per language code** — Meta does not translate content ("you are responsible for supplying strings ... in their appropriate language"), and same-name multi-language templates each count separately against the account's template limit and are each independently auto-reviewed/approved. There is no single template with sub-translations. [Meta — message templates](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/business-management-api/message-templates/)
- **Consumer WhatsApp app** defaults to device/OS locale; Android 13+ and iOS additionally expose a per-app "Change app language" override in WhatsApp Settings, independent of system locale — the same override-after-detect pattern recommended for Suwappu's bot. [WhatsApp FAQ](https://faq.whatsapp.com/779773243128935/?cms_platform=android)
- **Suwappu relevance**: Suwappu's own `bot/services/whatsapp_service.py` and `bot/services/whatsapp_templates.py` already pass a `language_code` per template (`bot/services/whatsapp_service.py:232-237`) — this matches Meta's documented per-language-template model exactly; no architecture change needed there, just coverage.

#### 2.2.2 New in v2 — Discord deep dive

- **Discord's own client** ships in ~30 languages, selectable in User Settings; historically ran a community-contributed translation portal that was later replaced primarily by professional/vendor translators (per a community support-forum thread that itself couldn't be directly fetched — flagged `UNVERIFIED` for exact current professional/community mix).
- **Application Command Localization API** (relevant to any Suwappu Discord-bot ambitions): `name_localizations`/`description_localizations` dictionaries on commands, subcommands/options, and choices; partial localization is allowed (missing locales fall back to the default). Critically, **the interaction payload the bot receives always uses the default, non-localized command name** — localization only changes what renders in the Discord client UI. Separately, every `Interaction` carries `locale` (invoker's client language) and `guild_locale` (server's `preferred_locale`) for picking localized *response* text at runtime — a distinct mechanism from command-name localization. [Discord — Application Commands](https://docs.discord.com/developers/interactions/application-commands), [Discord — Receiving/Responding to Interactions](https://docs.discord.com/developers/interactions/receiving-and-responding)
- **Bot examples**: MEE6's dashboard ships in ~5 languages; its AI Characters feature explicitly warns the underlying model is English-trained and recommends prompting "speaks only [language]" — an honest vendor admission that LLM-driven bot text needs explicit steering. Pokétwo runs a public Crowdin project for community-crowdsourced bot-string translation (closest analog to Discord's old community-portal model). Dyno's localization approach is UNVERIFIED — no public docs found. [MEE6 help](https://help.mee6.xyz/support/solutions/articles/101000549629-how-to-make-mee6-ai-characters-speak-in-other-languages), [Pokétwo on Crowdin](https://crowdin.com/project/poketwo)

### 2.3 Translation Management Tooling & Workflows

- **Convergent CI/CD loop across all major TMS vendors**: webhook on push/PR detects new/changed keys → sync to platform → MT/AI first-pass draft → human/community review → platform opens a return PR with translated resources → normal CI/tests → merge. [Crowdin GitHub Action](https://github.com/crowdin/github-action), [Lokalise blog](https://lokalise.com/blog/how-to-continuously-localize-your-front-end-resource-files-using-github-actions/)
- **Vendor positioning**: Crowdin owns "engineering-led" continuous localization (CLI scored 4.6/5 in a 2025 developer survey); Lokalise is the SaaS/product-team default; Phrase/Smartling target enterprise governance and managed linguist services; Transifex sits in between and trails on developer-experience satisfaction (3.8/5 vs Crowdin's lead). [Phrase blog comparison](https://phrase.com/blog/posts/localization-platform-comparison-2026/), [G2 comparison](https://www.g2.com/compare_reports/lokalise-smartling-crowdin-transifex-phrase-tms-formerly-memsource-and-phrase-strings)
- **A no-platform alternative exists**: an AI GitHub Action translates directly inside the PR diff and auto-commits to the branch for staging review — appropriate for small teams not ready to pay for a TMS seat. [Localhero.ai](https://localhero.ai/blog/automate-i18n-github-actions)
- **MT/LLM as first-pass draft, human polish for customer-facing copy** is the universal pattern: Lokalise auto-selects the best MT/LLM engine per language pair with project glossary/TM injected; Crowdin drafts from TM + DeepL/Google + OpenAI/Anthropic/Azure. [Lokalise blog](https://lokalise.com/blog/what-is-the-best-llm-for-translation/), [Crowdin docs](https://support.crowdin.com/machine-translation/)
- **Translation memory + glossary** underpin consistency: TM surfaces fuzzy matches for speed, glossaries lock brand/product terms and can be injected directly into MT output. [Smartling — TM](https://www.smartling.com/blog/what-is-translation-memory)
- **Uniswap/Crowdin claim — CORRECTED in v2 (was fact-checked as PARTIALLY VERIFIED):** v1 stated Uniswap's Interface runs "community draft → professional review → ship" via Crowdin. Adversarial fact-check confirms the Crowdin project (`crowdin.com/project/uniswap-interface`) is real and does sync to GitHub, **but no primary source supports a "professional review" step** — Crowdin's own project page describes only "join Crowdin to proofread existing translations or ask to join as a translator in the Discord" (community-translate → community-proofread, not professional review). We do not accept pull requests," and the automated `l10n_crowdin_action` sync branch was last updated June 2023 — suggesting the pipeline may be stale/inactive today, not actively staffed. **Corrected framing: Uniswap is evidence that a public Crowdin project for a DeFi interface is viable to set up, not evidence of an ongoing professional-review-gated pipeline.** [Crowdin — Uniswap Interface](https://crowdin.com/project/uniswap-interface), [Uniswap/interface CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/Uniswap/interface/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md), [l10n branch (stale, June 2023)](https://github.com/Uniswap/interface/branches/all?query=l10n)

#### 2.3.2 New in v2 — Is LLM translation actually good enough? (directly affects §4.4)

**Yes, with one hard caveat: placeholder/ICU-token corruption must be automatically validated, not trusted blindly.** Quality now matches or beats DeepL/Google NMT on most language pairs:

- Lokalise's blind 2025 study (professional translators rating output) rated **Claude 3.5 "good" more often than GPT-4, DeepL, or Google Translate.** [Lokalise](https://lokalise.com/blog/what-is-the-best-llm-for-translation/)
- Intento's *State of Translation Automation 2025* found GPT-4.1 and Claude Opus 4 now outperform NMT engines on most professional-translation tasks; DeepL still edges ahead specifically on French/German idiom handling. [machinetranslation.com](https://www.machinetranslation.com/blog/best-language-translation-api)
- Gemini 2.5 Pro topped the WMT 2025 shared-task rankings, ahead of DeepL/Google NMT. [blog.laratranslate.com](https://blog.laratranslate.com/translation-model-benchmark/)
- Caveat: these are **general-MT benchmarks, not UI-string-specific** — no published study isolating short, context-poor UI-string translation quality was found (UNVERIFIED for that narrower claim).
- **Speed/cost**: NMT (DeepL/Google) returns results in milliseconds — up to ~20x faster than LLMs (seconds) — which matters for real-time translation but not for batch/CI-driven localization pipelines like the one recommended in §4.4. DeepL API Pro is ≈$25/million characters flat; LLM pricing is token-based and messier to compare apples-to-apples (GPT-4o-mini ≈$0.15/M input tokens, GPT-4-class ≈$10/M) but is often cheaper at low/mid volume once output tokens and glossary-context tokens are included. [getblend.com](https://www.getblend.com/blog/which-llm-is-best-for-translation/), [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/deepl-pricing)
- **The real risk is placeholder/ICU-token corruption**: "a linguistically correct translation can fail by breaking critical placeholders." Crowdin AI addresses this with a self-correction layer diffing output against glossary/technical constraints; GPT-4o/5 is reported more reliable than average at preserving `{placeholders}`, but **no vendor claims 100%.** [Crowdin — AI localization pipelines](https://crowdin.com/blog/mastering-ai-localization-pipelines)
- **Practical setup**: (1) inject only the glossary terms present in each source string, not the whole termbase, to reduce prompt confusion; (2) always run automated placeholder-diff validation (regex-match ICU/format tokens pre- vs post-translation) before human review; (3) treat LLM output as pre-translation for humans, never final copy. [Lokalise — AI translation glossary](https://lokalise.com/blog/ai-translation-glossary/)
- **Net effect on Suwappu's recommendation (§4.4)**: this **confirms, not just permits,** the "AI-in-CI, LLM first-pass" approach as sound engineering — but it upgrades the requirement from "human review is mandatory" to "**automated placeholder-diff validation gate, then human review, is mandatory**" — a cheap CI check that catches the one documented LLM failure mode before a human even looks at it.

### 2.5 Technical Library Patterns Across a Polyglot Stack

| Layer | Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Python | **gettext/Babel** (`.po`/`.mo`, `pybabel extract`) | Mature, stdlib-adjacent, but plural syntax is C-style `ngettext`, not true ICU (see Appendix A) |
| Python (alt) | **Project Fluent** (`fluent.runtime`) | Own syntax purpose-built to avoid leaky placeholders, CLDR-plural-compatible, smaller ecosystem |
| Node/TS (flexible) | **i18next** / react-i18next | Most ecosystem-rich (detectors, backends, namespaces); native plural syntax is i18next-specific (`_one`/`_other` key suffixes), not raw ICU (bridge plugin `i18next-icu` exists) |
| Node/TS (ICU-strict) | **react-intl / FormatJS** | ICU-first, standards-strict, best for shared ICU catalogs across platforms |
| Next.js | **next-intl** | Next-native, ICU-based, lighter than react-intl, separate API/maintainer despite the naming similarity |
| React Native/Expo | **expo-localization** + i18next/react-i18next | Device locale/calendar/RTL signals + same runtime API as web, easing shared-package reuse |

- ICU MessageFormat is the shared syntax underlying react-intl/FormatJS, Vue-i18n, Symfony, Android quantity strings, iOS stringsdict — driven by Unicode CLDR plural rules; MessageFormat 2 (CLDR-stable as of March 2025) is a future successor, not yet the safe default (see Appendix A). [SimpleLocalize](https://simplelocalize.io/blog/posts/what-is-icu/), [Fluent vs ICU wiki](https://github.com/projectfluent/fluent/wiki/Fluent-and-ICU-MessageFormat)
- **Monorepo sharing pattern**: a `packages/i18n`-style package holding JSON/ICU catalogs keyed by **stable semantic IDs** (e.g. `wallet.created.title`), not source text — enables reuse across web/mobile/showcase without app recompilation. [Turborepo i18n discussion](https://github.com/i18next/i18next/discussions/1604)
- **Server- vs client-driven**: client-driven (ship locale JSON, translate in-app) is the majority React/Next pattern and CDN-cacheable; server-driven (API returns pre-rendered strings) suits dynamic/transactional content where the caller has no locale files. A mixed strategy — client for UI chrome, server for transactional copy — is common. [Locize — server-side i18n](https://www.locize.com/blog/how-does-server-side-internationalization-look-like/)

## Appendix A: New in v2 — ICU/CLDR Plural Technical Detail

**Bottom line: Suwappu's bot currently has no plural system at all** — `bot/i18n.py` is a hand-rolled dict of `.format()` strings (confirmed again in this v2 audit, `bot/i18n.py:22-122`, re-verified against the live file). For correct multilingual pluralization anywhere in the stack, use **CLDR plural categories** as the data model and **ICU MessageFormat 1** (not MF2 yet) as the syntax.

- **CLDR plural categories**: up to six named tags — `zero, one, two, few, many, other` (`other` is always the required fallback). Category counts by language family: English/Spanish/French use 2 forms (`one`, `other`); Russian uses 4 (`one`, `few`, `many`, `other` — e.g. 1→one, 2-4→few, 5-20→many); Arabic uses all 6; Chinese/Japanese use just 1 (`other` only — no plural morphology).
- **ICU plural syntax** (FormatJS/react-intl):
  ```
  {count, plural,
    one {You have # new alert}
    other {You have # new alerts}
  }
  ```
  For Russian this expands to `one {}`/`few {}`/`many {}`/`other {}` branches; `#` inserts the formatted number. [formatjs.io — ICU syntax](https://formatjs.io/docs/core-concepts/icu-syntax)
- **MessageFormat 2 (MF2) status, precisely**: Unicode/CLDR 47 (March 2025) marked MF2 syntax stable/final. ICU4J/ICU4C ship tech-preview implementations; JS has a standalone `messageformat` 4.x parser. But **as of mid-2026, no major production i18n library defaults to it** — FormatJS/react-intl and i18next still default to ICU MF1; i18next's MF2 support is an opt-in plugin still mid-migration; TC39's `Intl.MessageFormat` proposal is stalled below Stage 2.7 pending broader adoption. **Recommendation: build on ICU MF1 now** — MF2 tooling isn't production-ready enough to bet a first i18n rollout on. [locize — MF2 in i18next](https://locize.com/blog/messageformat-2-i18next)
- **Why Python's gettext isn't the same thing**: `gettext.ngettext(singular, plural, n)` only ever picks between two hardcoded strings via a per-locale numeric *index* computed from a C-style boolean expression (Polish's `.po` header, e.g., is `nplurals=3; plural=(n==1?0:n%10>=2&&n%10<=4&&(n%100<10||n%100>=20)?1:2);`) — it returns an index, not a semantic CLDR tag. Babel *can* parse true CLDR rules, and `babel.plural.PluralRule.to_gettext()` converts named tags into that same C-expression for `.mo` compatibility — but a pure gettext workflow loses the `one/few/many/other` semantics unless the code stays inside Babel's own API rather than round-tripping through `.po` files. For a from-scratch bot, ICU-syntax + FormatJS-style category matching is more maintainable than gettext index-juggling. [python docs — gettext](https://docs.python.org/3/library/gettext.html), [babel — plural API](https://babel.pocoo.org/en/latest/api/plural.html)
- **Direct action for Suwappu**: replace `bot/i18n.py`'s dict-lookup format strings with CLDR-category plural handling (via `babel.support.Format`/`PluralRule`, or a Python ICU binding) for any count-bearing message (alert counts, order counts, XP totals, referral counts, confirmation counts). Add `one/few/many/other` branches for Russian if/when added, `one/other` for en/es/fr, `other`-only for zh.

## 4. Recommended Approach for Suwappu

### 4.1 Language Detection & Override

Adopt the pattern Suwappu's webapp and showcase already use, and finish it for the bot:

1. **Default**: Telegram's `user.language_code` (bot) / `initDataUnsafe.user.language_code` (webapp) — free, zero-friction, already implemented on two of three surfaces.
2. **Explicit override, always wins**: add a `/language` (or `/lang`) bot command with an inline-keyboard picker that writes to the existing `bot/models/user.py:39` `language_preference` column. `bot/i18n.py:get_user_lang()` already accepts a `stored_lang` parameter designed for exactly this — the fix is wiring callers to pass it, not changing the function's logic.
3. **Wire the webapp UI to the existing write path**: `api-ts`'s `UserService.updateUserPreferences()` (`api-ts/src/services/UserService.ts:161-162`) already accepts `languagePreference` — add the missing form field/call in `webapp/src/pages/Settings.tsx` so a webapp-set language actually persists server-side, not just to `localStorage`.
4. **Mobile (when built)**: `expo-localization`'s `getLocales()` for initial OS-locale signal, explicit in-app setting persisted via `AsyncStorage`/SecureStore taking priority thereafter — same override-before-detect ordering as the two web surfaces, for consistency across the fleet.

### 4.2 Where to Store Locale Strings

Replace the three-independent-catalog situation with **one shared source of truth**:

- Create a **shared locales package** — extend `packages/shared/` (e.g. `packages/shared/locales/`) holding JSON/ICU catalogs keyed by **stable semantic string IDs** (e.g. `wallet.created.title`, `swap.confirm.body`), not raw English text. This is consumed directly by `webapp` (i18next resource loader) and `mobile` (i18next), and mapped to `showcase`'s next-intl format via a small adapter (both are ICU-based, so the catalog content itself is portable even if the loader API differs).
- **Python bot equivalent**: since Python can't `import` a TS package, generate a parallel Python resource (JSON or `.po`/Fluent `.ftl` files) from the *same* canonical string-ID source at build/CI time — a single translation source, two runtime formats. This avoids the current situation where bot/webapp/showcase can silently disagree on a translated string for the same concept.
- Keep transactional/dynamic bot copy (tx status, fee amounts, confirmations) server-rendered as it is today — that's consistent with the "mixed strategy" pattern (client-driven for UI chrome, server-driven for dynamic content) used across the industry.

### 4.3 Library Choices Per Component

| Component | Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `bot/` (Python) | **Babel/gettext** (`.po`/`.mo`, `pybabel extract`) as the pragmatic default; consider **Fluent** (`fluent.runtime`) only if grammar-heavy target languages (Arabic, Russian plural forms) become priority | gettext is mature and low-friction for a small team; current hand-rolled dict has no plural support (Appendix A) and should be retired once volume grows past ~20-30 keys |
| `api-ts/` | No UI strings expected here (API layer) — but validate error/status messages returned to bot/webapp are string-ID based, not hardcoded English, if they're user-facing | Keeps server-driven dynamic copy consistent with the shared catalog |
| `webapp/` | Keep **i18next/react-i18next** (already in place) — add `Intl.NumberFormat`/`Intl.DateTimeFormat` for all balance/fee/date rendering; adopt Uniswap's `_one`/`_other` plural-key convention (§2.5.1) for count-bearing strings | Don't rip out a working library; close the CLDR-formatting and plural gaps instead |
| `showcase/` | Keep **next-intl** (already in place) | Working, Next-native, no reason to change |
| `mobile/` (when built) | **expo-localization + i18next/react-i18next** | Matches webapp's runtime API, maximizes shared-package reuse per the Expo/Phrase-documented pattern |

### 4.4 Lightweight Translation Workflow (small-team appropriate) — updated in v2

- **Start without a paid TMS. Use the "AI-in-CI" pattern** — a GitHub Action detects new/changed string-ID keys in the shared catalog on PR, runs an LLM (Claude or GPT-4o/5) translation pass with a maintained glossary (crypto terms: gas, slippage, bridge, staking — pin these to Coinbase/Binance-style canonical glossary translations) injected as context, and commits the draft translations back into the same PR for review before merge. **This is no longer just a cost-saving shortcut** — §2.3.2's LLM-vs-NMT research independently confirms LLM output now matches or beats DeepL/Google NMT quality on most language pairs (Lokalise's blind 2025 study rated Claude 3.5 above GPT-4/DeepL/Google Translate), so this recommendation is upgraded from "reasonable for a small team" to "evidence-backed as a legitimate first-pass method," not merely a stopgap.
- **New required step: automated placeholder/ICU-token validation before human review.** The one well-documented LLM translation failure mode is placeholder/format-token corruption (a fluent, grammatically correct translation that silently drops or reorders a `{amount}`/`{chain_name}` placeholder). Add a CI check that regex-diffs all `{placeholder}`/ICU tokens between source and translated string and **fails the PR** on mismatch — cheap to build, and the single highest-leverage guardrail given §2.3.2's findings. Do this *before* the human-review step, not instead of it.
- **Human review is still mandatory before merge for anything user-facing**, and **non-negotiable for money-path or compliance copy** (see Section 5) — a native or fluent speaker on the team (or a contracted reviewer) signs off, not just CI green plus the placeholder-diff check.
- **Maintain a small glossary file** in the shared locales package from day one — even 20-30 locked terms (swap, wallet, fee, slippage, gas, confirm, cancel) prevents drift across bot/webapp/showcase far more cheaply than post-hoc cleanup. Per §2.3.2, inject only the glossary terms actually present in each source string, not the whole termbase — reduces prompt confusion and cost.
- **Graduate to a TMS (Crowdin or Lokalise, or self-hosted Weblate/Tolgee — see §2.3.1's pricing table) once**: (a) string count crosses roughly a few hundred and manual PR review becomes a bottleneck, (b) more than 2-3 languages are live simultaneously, or (c) community/crowdsourced translation contribution is wanted. Crowdin (Pro, ~$59/mo) is the best-value graduation target given its native GitHub sync and Uniswap/Pokétwo/Binance-Academy precedent for crypto/community projects (§2.3.1) — but note the corrected Uniswap claim above: budget for an actual reviewer, don't assume a "professional review" step exists by default in a public Crowdin project.

### 4.5 Phased Rollout Plan

1. **Phase 0 (foundation, no new languages)**: unify the three catalogs into the shared `packages/shared/locales/` structure; wire the dormant bot `/language` command and the webapp Settings form field to the existing DB columns; add `Intl.NumberFormat` to all webapp balance/fee displays; add CI pseudo-localization as the default debug build (Netflix pattern, §2.6) while the catalog is still small and cheap to retrofit. No user-visible language change yet — this is de-risking existing infrastructure.
2. **Phase 1 (2-3 languages, soft launch behind a flag)**: pick languages by actual user base data (not assumption) — Suwappu already has es/fr/zh scaffolded across two of three surfaces, suggesting these are the existing bet; verify against real Telegram `language_code` distribution in production logs before committing further translation spend. Ship behind a feature flag to a small cohort first, consistent with the general feature-flag-gated staged-rollout pattern used industry-wide for de-risking any user-facing change. Extend bot coverage from 2 handlers to the high-traffic set: swap, balance, portfolio, wallet, alerts.
3. **Phase 2 (widen coverage, full handler set)**: once Phase 1 languages are stable and reviewed, extend to remaining bot handlers (`orders`, `snipe`, `ref`, `xp`, admin commands stay English-only as internal tooling) and reassess whether a TMS is now justified per the graduation criteria above.
4. **Phase 3 (RTL, if/when Arabic/Hebrew markets are targeted)**: add pseudo-localization (`en-XA`/`ar-XB`-style build flag) to CI *before* real Arabic/Hebrew translation work starts, to catch webapp layout mirroring and text-expansion bugs early — there is currently zero RTL precedent in this codebase, so this needs to be planned as its own workstream, not bolted onto an existing language launch.

## 5. Money-Path Risks Specific to Suwappu

**MONEY-PATH — flag any implementation touching these for `money-path-reviewer` per repo convention.**

- **Mistranslated amounts/fees/confirmation messages**: Suwappu's bot renders swap confirmations, fee breakdowns, and balance figures through `bot/i18n.py`'s `.format()`-based templates. A translation error here (wrong placeholder order, mistranslated "fee" vs "amount" in a target language) can cause a user to confirm a swap they misunderstood — this is a direct financial-harm vector, not a cosmetic bug. Any new-language addition touching `bot/i18n.py` confirmation/fee strings, or webapp's transaction-summary copy, needs `money-path-reviewer` sign-off, matching the fintech research finding that regulated/financial copy is a distinct translation tier requiring higher scrutiny than marketing UI. [Centific](https://www.centific.com/blog/financial-services-localization-compliance)
- **Decimal separator locale confusion**: no confirmed `Intl.NumberFormat` usage in `webapp/src/lib/i18n.ts` or nearby balance/amount components today. Locales that use comma-as-decimal (much of the EU, Latin America) risk users misreading `1.234,56` vs `1,234.56` style ambiguity in raw string formatting, and — **confirmed** via `DeFiChain #176` — display truncation diverging from the actual on-chain value has caused a real MAX-send "Invalid amount" bug in a production wallet elsewhere. This is the single highest-priority gap: fix number formatting via CLDR-backed `Intl.NumberFormat` **before** shipping additional languages to money-facing screens. [DeFiChain #176 — verified](https://github.com/BirthdayResearch/defichain-app/issues/176)
- **Pluralization bugs in critical confirmation flows**: neither the bot's hand-rolled dict nor webapp/showcase's i18next/next-intl catalogs currently demonstrate ICU-correct plural handling (§3, Appendix A). A string like "You will receive {n} token(s)" naively pluralized breaks in languages with more than two plural forms (e.g. Russian, Arabic) — if such a string appears in a swap-confirmation or withdrawal flow, a broken plural could read as a different quantity than intended. Any pluralized string on a confirm/execute button path is money-path-adjacent and should get review.
- **RTL layout issues in webapp for Arabic/Hebrew markets**: not yet applicable (no RTL languages live), but flagged proactively — if Arabic/Hebrew is added, un-mirrored balance/fee layout in the webapp (e.g. amount-then-currency-symbol ordering, transaction direction arrows, swap-from/swap-to positioning) could visually invert a swap direction or amount grouping for an RTL reader. This must be pseudo-localization-tested (Section 4.5, Phase 3) before real Arabic/Hebrew translation ships to any transaction screen.
- **Cross-stack consistency risk**: because bot (`bot/i18n.py`), webapp (`webapp/src/locales/`), and showcase (`showcase/messages/`) are three independently-maintained catalogs today, the same financial concept (e.g. "slippage tolerance") could already be translated inconsistently across surfaces with no automated check catching the drift — this is a correctness/trust risk even before any new-language work begins, and argues for the Phase 0 catalog-unification work happening ahead of, not alongside, new-language rollout. **New in v2, concretely confirmed**: `showcase/messages/en.json` already ships live "Earn 30% of every trading fee" referral copy in 4 languages (§3) — this is a real, already-shipped instance of the exact risk this bullet warns about, not a hypothetical future one.

### New in v2 — Precedent: documented localization/translation risk at crypto exchanges

No specific, dated, named incident of a mistranslated fee/risk disclosure causing quantifiable user harm at a major exchange (Binance, OKX, Coinbase, Kraken, Bybit) was found in this research pass — that absence is reported honestly, not papered over. What *is* documented, and stands as concrete structural precedent for why Suwappu should treat this as a real risk rather than a theoretical one:

- **OKX maintains a standing legal disclaimer specifically about machine-translation risk**, stating "no automated translation is flawless," that English is the sole binding legal text, and that "any discrepancies or differences resulting from translation ... do not hold legal weight for compliance or enforcement purposes." A major exchange felt the need to write this disclaimer — implying the underlying risk (a translated disclosure diverging from the source in a way a user could rely on) is real enough to warrant a standing legal hedge. [OKX — disclaimer for machine translation](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/disclaimer-for-machine-translation)
- **Binance's community Translation Bounty Program explicitly bans machine-translated submissions and disqualifies low-quality translators** — a rule that only makes sense in response to an actual, experienced quality problem with amateur/MT-only crypto-content translation. [Binance Academy Translation Bounty](https://www.binance.com/en/blog/all/binance-academy-translation-bounty--round-2-287503278263664640)
- **Read together**, these two data points support the same conclusion as the DeFiChain #176 wallet bug and the MiCA cross-jurisdiction divergence (§2.4): localization in crypto/fintech is a documented, exchange-acknowledged risk surface, even where a single headline "incident" isn't publicly reported. For Suwappu specifically, this means: (1) never rely on raw LLM/MT output for disclosure-adjacent or fee-confirmation copy without the placeholder-diff + human-review gate in §4.4, and (2) consider adding Suwappu's own equivalent of OKX's disclaimer (e.g., "English is authoritative; translated confirmations are provided for convenience") once non-English confirmation copy ships to production, as a direct mitigant modeled on documented exchange practice.
